# Whole-request document discourse

Owner: Codex document lane. This successor builds on the source-witness packet
committed in `de672b1`. The new native returns source quotations for whole-topic
discourse, including “The Cure” and “The Band”. It replaces the unsafe legacy
convenient-word Statement admission. Ten theorem scans pass with zero oracles and
25 computed checks; actual consumer results and their limits appear below.

## Behavior being replaced

The old converse organ picks a convenient word from a request and returns its
definition. Actual examples include “Tell me the capital of Chile” answered with
a definition of Chile, and “The director of the film Ran” answered with a definition
of director. Citation and quotation preservation do not make those replies answers
to the requested relation.

The recorded core panel has ten relation-shaped `organ:converse>statement` items.
Seven returned nonresponsive definitions; three supplied responsive country
descriptions. All ten now clarify. This removes seven incorrect admissions and
loses three useful replies until the relation reader supports their complete
property/subject forms. The clarification count is not an answerability gain.

## Whole topic and source sentence

The new `EDiscourse` case supports four complete request forms:
“I am interested in <topic>”, “I like <topic>”, “Tell me about <topic>”, and
“I want to learn about <topic>”. The topic includes any article in its title.
This matters in the actual source: `The Cure` and `Cure` are different pages,
as are `The Band` and `Band`. Dropping “The” before lookup would silently change
the subject. “Tell me about a volcano” therefore need not resolve when “a volcano”
is not an indexed title; the supported “Tell me about volcano” can use the source
sentence beginning “A volcano ...”. No convenient-word fallback is added.

The existing extracted decision checks the reconstructed whole request, all
matching title witnesses, nonblank agreeing page/title identifiers, both row-page
identifiers, a nonblank citation, and a literal subject plus definition clause.
Present and past copula forms (`is/are/was/were a/an/the`) and `refers to` are the
finite supported clauses. The selected source sentence is quoted with its citation.
The same source-witness, quotation, and conditional snapshot theorems cover this
additional case; adding a grammar alternative does not justify inflating the
number of independent semantic guarantees.

Exact alias-to-title identity, complete matching-alias fetches, and source-row
authenticity remain explicit assumptions. Source factual truth and general
natural-language entailment are not proved. The definition syntax is deliberately
finite; unfamiliar paraphrases, missing titles and unresolved subjects can withhold.

## Fallback reference correction

Full-dispatch inspection found “A square has side length 2 m. What is its area
in cm2?” reaching the document fallback and being told that conversation history
was missing. The previous fetch proposed a pointer for any occurrence of `its`,
`this`, or even “the city”; that does not establish an absent referent.
The untrusted proposal now accepts only complete reference-only forms such as
“What is it?”, “What is its area?” and “Describe that”. It cannot select an
incidental pronoun in an explicit multi-clause request. The existing kernel still
checks that the proposed pointer occurs; this change adds no HOL entailment claim.
Shared dialogue handles its supported contextual bindings before the fallback.

`var/document/reference-only-final.json` records 4/4 actual frontdoor checks:
the square and two explicit Lyon requests reach ordinary clarification, while
“What is it?” retains the specific reference clarification. Six proposal controls
also pass, including suffixes that name an explicit subject. This is a correction
to a fallback diagnostic, not a solution of the square calculation. The initial
six-consumer probe expected two earlier captured routes to reach this terminal;
those expectations did not hold (`reference-only-check.json`). The final check
separates pointer proposals from actual terminal routing instead of claiming that
all reference-shaped input reaches the terminal. The 38-case composition receipt
above predates this narrow fetch correction; these four consumer checks exercise
its changed behavior.

## Theorem statements and remaining scope

For any `AnchoredAnswer`, the extracted decision guarantees a supported complete
request, agreement of all supplied matching aliases, and a supplied sentence row
whose page, source page, title and nonblank citation match the selected subject.
The row starts with the permitted literal subject and finite clause syntax.
The rendered reply contains the selected source sentence. Under the separate
premises that supplied matching aliases equal the snapshot's matching aliases
and supplied rows are authentic snapshot rows, the selected witness is a member
of that snapshot and all its matching aliases agree. These are conditional
source and syntactic support claims, not general referent or factual entailment.
The ten existing scans also retain the earlier event/date clause guarantees;
EDiscourse adds no event-date rule.

Legacy definition admission and the heavy untrusted bridge still have their
previous limits. Summary, timeline and simple-explanation captures still
withhold; this change does not complete those capabilities. A new grammar case
must preserve the original request and bind actual selected source evidence.
