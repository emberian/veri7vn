## Checked computation and assumptions

The separate query constraint interpreter checks instance ancestry, or an exact
property/object-ancestry witness. A novel can be established by P31 ancestry or
P136 genre ancestry. Frankenstein’s stored P31 is literary work and its P136 is
an epistolary novel; the implementation checks that separate genre row and never
invents a novel instance row. Before filtering, **every original candidate** must
have the required finite classification/row/closure evidence available. Missing
alternative evidence cannot manufacture uniqueness.

`queried_forward_answer` states: if the actual `decide_query` returns an answer
and all supplied attempts are forward, there is an original supplied attempt
and candidate satisfying the interpreted query predicate and specialist domain,
with the exact nonblank answer row and answered subject ID; every original
candidate of that answering attempt passed `query_known`. `query_genre_witness`
connects a genre match to its exact property/object row and supplied object
ancestry. The other scanned statements connect scoped membership, forward
identity behavior, and unchanged AnyQuery/inverse behavior. The main decision
consumes this interpreter; query-filtered IDs also supply conversation metadata.

English recognition, entity linking, SQLite row completeness/authenticity and
source ontology meaning/coverage remain premises. The city vocabulary explicitly
names generic/big/port/US/Chile/Japan city classes whose labels were inspected,
because the source omits some P279 edges even for big/port cities. Town and
national-capital alone are insufficient. This finite vocabulary is not a proof
of complete real-world city classification. Typed answers do not inherit the
older claim that all *unfiltered* linked candidates agree.
