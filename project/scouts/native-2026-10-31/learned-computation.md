# Learned computations that Oracle can actually use

31 October 2026 · learned-computation scout · research/design only.
**No specialist panels, model calls, training, builds or tests ran; no panel numbers exist for this lane.**

A learned readout can be a useful new computation even when we cannot write its symbolic algorithm or explain the base model's decoder. I recommend a supplied-document binding readout first, followed by typed program search. Representation mining should earn its keep in those consumers rather than become an independent feature-description factory.

## Current boundary: programs exist; unrestricted language understanding does not

`native/oracle-core/src/program.rs` already supplies a closed 27-operation KQA language: `Find`, `Relate`, concept/value filters, set combinations, projections, comparisons and qualifiers. `Program::parse/validate` checks arities, preceding dependencies, types, literals and even unused branches; `Operation::contract` exposes the grammar. Its own documentation correctly calls this an executable contract, not a proof of the upstream interpreter. Valid programs can still express the wrong question.

`native/oracle-core/src/reader.rs::evaluate_whole/evaluate_inner` recognizes whole templates, exact fragments/segments and one nested pass. `ReaderReading` retains program, answer and execution evidence; `ReaderResult` retains alternatives. The final decision applies the source form guard to **all** successful readings: a vetoed alternative cannot disappear so another becomes uniquely acceptable. Unsupported programs, ambiguous execution and answer disagreement prevent answering. Bounds are host policy, not learned confidence.

`native/oracle-host/src/native.rs` exposes `kqa.query` and `kqa.execute`; its reader adapter converts results to `front::Candidate`. `front.rs` arbitrates groups by agreement/refusal/unavailability, not confidence ranking. Therefore an empirical estimate must not silently become an ordinary sourced-fact candidate: its meaning and uncertainty need an explicit operation/result boundary. `tools/oracle_workbench/operations.py` is an existing structured supplied-data provider; `workbench.documents.query` quotes matching passages. It is a consumer precedent, not evidence of a native learned-operation provider already existing.

The [27 October report](../../lanes/LITERATURE-NEUROSYMBOLIC-2026-10-27.md) already distinguishes readout utility, causal explanation and correctness, and proposes date estimation and typed parsing. This report adds discourse binding and utility-directed representation mining against the current native IR. None is claimed novel in the literature.

## 1. Compute a binding table from supplied notes

Proposed operation `representations.discourse_bindings` takes supplied passages and a requested relation vocabulary. It returns empirical `(entity span, relation, attribute span, polarity, attribution, time scope)` records, competing bindings, confidence/abstention and exact source offsets. The useful new function is tracking who did/owned/approved what across clauses, pronouns and repeated entities. A user can inspect a responsibility table from meeting notes or compare changing ownership across incident reports. A specialist can consume this table for an explicitly conditional join or chronology: “assuming these inferred bindings.” Learned extraction remains useful even if its mechanism is unknown.

[Dai, Heinzerling and Inui, ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2194/) supplies a concrete representation hypothesis: PLS decodes entity×relation indices from attribute activations; subspace patching changes relational predictions. Sections 3.1–3.3, limitations and Appendix A.2 were read. Their indices denote order in a discourse, **not durable entity IDs or Wikidata predicates**. Start with request-local span pointers and explicit relation labels; maintain ambiguity rather than merging by vector proximity. Their controlled synthetic narratives and one-token inventories do not establish real-document performance, role shifts or long-context robustness.

Begin with 1,200 controlled passages from fresh randomized binding tables plus 300 independently annotated human passages, subject to permission/licensing. These are proposed sizes. Split whole generation templates, names, relation families and document origins before representation selection. Hold out an entire relation family and an independently written prose collection; aliases and paraphrases of one table stay together. Annotators label unsupported/contradicted/ambiguous separately. Synthetic labels test exact controlled semantics; they cannot substitute for human-prose validation.

Fit frozen-activation linear/PLS span-pair readouts before a small nonlinear head. Compare lexical patterns, token/position-only features, a frozen embedding classifier and generated model answers. Measure complete-table accuracy, tuple precision/recall, wrong binding rate at fixed answered coverage and calibration by held-out group. Challenge role reversal, same-name entities, quoted assertions, negation, reassignment, absent facts, multi-token names and adversarial entity ordering. A pointer proves where text occurs, not that the inferred relation follows from it. Never derive world-level absence or uniqueness from an omitted predicted row.

Reject this consumer if gains disappear on human prose, position-only controls match it, or selective error fails the predeclared usefulness threshold. Keep the existing passage reader available. No causal explanation is needed to accept the readout as an empirical operation.

## 2. Learn which typed programs to explore

Use the existing `ProgramStep` representation directly. A frozen question encoder plus trained heads predicts operation, dependency/type, literal span and alternative probabilities; a bounded search enumerates admissible completions and executes against one immutable image. A model proposes no SQL, host method, executable code, coverage flag or mutable selection. Runtime owns validation, cancellation and resource accounting; a separate proposal module owns model state and scores. A trained embedding can also cluster reusable program fragments for library/search guidance, as in [DreamCoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381), already in the existing paperbin.

Concrete consumer: questions with familiar predicates in unseen compositions, currently missed by exact templates, including qualifier/date-role and inverse-relation requests. Search can share the existing execution DAG. First use ranking only within a finite candidate set and finish the same admitted alternatives: answer behavior should remain unchanged, but reordering exhaustive agreement alone does not reduce total work. Measure possible latency, locality, shared-subproblem reuse and earlier refutation benefits separately; less execution requires a sound early-termination rule or actual sharing, not ranking confidence. Expanding to genuinely new parses is a separate empirical coverage experiment and cannot prove completeness. Coordinate with the reader scout on a packed derivation forest carrying an explicit complete/exhausted distinction; apply its plural-first grammar-data correction before attributing gains to architecture. Top-k agreement, or an exhausted neural search, cannot establish all plausible meanings agree. Expose remaining alternatives and bounded-search incompleteness; source-form vetoes still apply.

Propose 1,000 manually checked question/program pairs, augmented with generated type-correct contrasts and unsupported questions. Split by canonical program skeleton, entity group and predicate composition, not surface string. Freeze a separate author-written intent set. Report semantic program accuracy, intent-equivalent alternatives, right/wrong/withheld after execution, search expansions, latency and peak memory. Include valid-but-wrong examples: author versus composer, event date versus publication date, inverse edges and count over incomplete coverage. Execution returning a plausible value is not supervision that a parse was intended.

If useful task representations emerge, assign latent blocks for relation, entity pointer, qualifier and direction, then compare task-only training, supervised blocks and interchange-intervention training (IIT). The existing [Geiger et al. paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/geiger22a.html), equations 1–3 and IIT objective, defines counterfactual swaps; it distinguishes exhaustive interchange agreement from sampled accuracy. Swap a relation block while preserving the base entity/direction, using the typed program to define the expected counterfactual. Reserve unseen source/base pairs and combinations; test off-target changes and residual bypass. A high-capacity alignment can learn the task itself: compare linear alignments, random/shuffled variables and restricted capacity. Report causal fidelity separately from parse utility. Reject added structure if it costs useful accuracy without improving held-out intervention behavior or search.

## 3. Mine representations for useful support and contrast

A second direct empirical consumer, `representations.relation_support`, computes whether a supplied passage supports, contradicts or leaves unresolved a proposed scoped relation. It helps users find disagreements between documents, including a claim with negation or changed attribution that shares nearly every word. This advances beyond lexical fidelity checks without claiming a semantic-equivalence theorem. Initially reuse binding-task labels plus independently reviewed contradiction/quantifier contrasts; keep whole documents and relation families out of training. Evaluate macro scores **and** false-support risk at fixed coverage against lexical and dense-readout baselines. If the existing dense head wins, ship that research result; sparse explanations are optional.

Mine SAE/subspace features only to improve this computation or select hard exemplars for program learning. [Hao et al., PULSE, September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32469), sections 3–5.2, measures paired demonstration utility rather than generic similarity; it uses 64 discovery queries and 32 candidate sets each, holds evaluation apart and ablates raw/PCA/SAE bases. Its transfer is partial and dataset-conditioned. An Oracle adaptation would label utility by **independent scoped-relation or semantic-program correctness**, not model confidence, fluent explanations or repeated exam success. Compare utility-selected exemplars with random, lexical, dense and matched-compute retrieval. Its demonstrated setting is ICL exemplar retrieval; extending it to Oracle's program search is a hypothesis.

[Gerasimov et al., June 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12138), sections 3, 7–8 and limitations, finds unstable individual features can occupy reproducible subspaces. Require multiple dictionary seeds and subspace/task stability before giving features durable names; random-model controls can still receive plausible automatic descriptions. Existing [SAEBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09532), sections 4.6–5, reinforces downstream evaluation and limited ground-truth coverage. [SASA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333) is retained as a subspace-architecture lead only: the downloaded PDF yielded abstract/introduction text but not the full body through extraction. Its theorem assumptions were not reviewed; no architecture choice rests on those claims.

## Order, cost and decision boundary

First agree on span/attribution/time-scope schemas with reader, relations and native-operation scouts, and obtain labels. Then freeze holdouts and evaluate the readout and candidate-ranking baselines independently. Only afterward investigate IIT or SAE mining. Use an open-weight model with hooks; embedding APIs support vector readouts but not internal patching or IIT. Text-only APIs cannot supply activations.

Cost comes mainly from activation collection, not fitting linear heads. Proposed first cap: 2 million token-forwards, at most three selected layers, cached pooled/span states instead of full residual histories; CPU heads and no base-model updates. At 20,000 pooled states × 4,096 dimensions × two bytes, one layer is about 164 MB before metadata; tokenwise caching costs far more. Benchmark throughput on the eventual authorized host before assigning GPU-hours or money. No model/host/resource authorization is implied here. IIT adds paired forward/backward work; defer it until a measured pilot supports a capped adapter run. Reuse a compatible published SAE first. Training dictionaries across many seeds, or reproducing the seed paper's billion-token setting, is outside this initial budget.

Three claims remain independent: empirical usefulness requires untouched task outcomes; causal interpretation requires controlled original-model interventions; formal correctness covers explicit program/checker premises, never the neural reading or completeness of source data. Final runtime selection needs shipped specialist six-criterion panels plus independent truth cases. This scout supplies none of those results.
